Why I Gave Up Trying to Write Articles
Somewhere in the first couple of dozen, the ones that worked were those that refused to perform “writer".

This is my hundredth.

I only mention the number because it is almost beside the point. What actually happened is quieter and more useful. Somewhere in the first couple of dozen pieces I noticed a clean distinction. Certain ones felt like work. They required a kind of deliberate arrangement, a careful arranging of sentences into something that looked like an article. They sat on the screen and felt slightly dead, as if I had assembled them rather than found them. The others did not feel like work at all. They felt as if they had already been written somewhere inside and I was simply typing them onto the page. The difference was not effort in the ordinary sense. It was the difference between performing the role of someone who writes articles and simply talking.

Once I saw that distinction I stopped trying to write articles.

The early ones that felt like work had a particular texture. They were tidy. They had clear openings and tidy conclusions. They used the sort of measured, slightly elevated language that signals “this is a proper piece of writing.” They were not bad in any technical sense. They just felt lifeless in the way a conversation feels lifeless when both people are performing the idea of a good conversation instead of actually having one. I would finish them and feel a small, private dissatisfaction that I could not quite name at the time. It was the dissatisfaction of having completed a task rather than having said something.

The shift was not dramatic. There was no single moment of revelation. It was more like noticing that the pieces which people stayed with, the ones that gathered a few quiet reads and the occasional small signal of recognition, were the ones in which I had simply said what was on my mind in the voice I actually have. The humour was left in where it belonged. The digressions were left in. The private thoughts that would normally be edited out for the sake of polish were left in. Nothing was hidden in the name of sounding like a writer. Those pieces did not feel like work while I was making them. They felt like transcription.

That is the practical test I still use. If the next sentence requires me to decide how a proper article would continue, I am probably performing. If the next sentence is already present and I am only deciding whether to type it or not, I am probably doing the other thing.

Over time the individual pieces began to show a quiet architecture. Each one has a recognisable internal shape: a title that does a particular kind of work, a first line that either catches or does not, a body that moves in a certain rhythm, a closing cadence that feels like the natural end of a conversation rather than a formal conclusion. Different pieces carry different kinds of signal. Some are closer to project notes. Some are closer to theoretical exploration. Some are simply the record of something that was interesting that week. None of them were written to form a larger system, yet taken together they have begun to behave like one. The overall presence is simply the ongoing conversation that results when the same person keeps talking, in the same voice, about the things that are actually alive for them. That is as close as I come to a modular description, and I will leave it there. The architecture is quiet and mostly underneath. It does not need to be announced.

There is one small practical consequence of the way this has developed. I have never monetised the writing. The decision was not made as a grand statement. It was made because the moment you begin to optimise for the Partner Program, or for any other external return, a slight pressure appears to shape the next piece toward what might perform. That pressure is subtle, but it is exactly the pressure that turns transcription back into assembly. The cost is real and ordinary: you do not get to plug the other things you make, you do not get to turn the writing into a sales channel, you do not get the small monthly numbers that some people reasonably enjoy. The quieter return is that the signal stays unbent. You get to keep developing this slightly odd little form for its own sake. Medium articles, when they work, are a specific art. They are not essays in the old sense, not blog posts in the old sense, and not magazine features. They are a short, intimate, slightly conspiratorial form that lives or dies by whether the reader feels they are being spoken to rather than addressed. The form only stays healthy if you refuse to write it as a form.

I am not offering this as a method. Methods are what the lifeless pieces were full of. I am only describing what happened when I stopped trying to produce the appearance of writing and simply continued the conversation that was already underway. The metrics that people usually care about — steady reads, the people who return, the small signals that someone stayed to the end — have followed in the ordinary way that consequences follow causes. They are not the point. They are downstream of the decision to keep the writing from feeling like work.

If there is a concrete takeaway, it is only this. Write the thing that is actually present. Use the voice you actually have. Leave the humour where it belongs and the digressions where they belong. If a piece begins to feel like assembly rather than transcription, stop and ask what you would say if no one were going to read it. Then say that. The rest tends to arrange itself.

You can get here too. Not by following a sequence of steps, and not by performing the role of someone who has arrived, but by refusing, again and again, to write the article you think you are supposed to write. Keep doing that long enough and one day you look up and notice the number. The number is almost beside the point. The conversation is not.
